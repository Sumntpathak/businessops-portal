## Skilled migration: how it usually works
1. The occupation must be on a skilled occupation list.
2. Skills assessment by the assessing authority for that occupation.
3. English test, such as IELTS or PTE.
4. Points test: at least 65 points, from age, English, work experience, qualifications and more.
5. Submit an Expression of Interest in SkillSelect and wait for an invitation.
6. Once invited, lodge the visa application.

## Skilled visas (no employer needed)
- Skilled Independent, subclass 189: permanent, points-tested, no sponsor.
- Skilled Nominated, subclass 190: permanent, needs nomination by a state or territory.
- Skilled Work Regional, subclass 491: provisional for 5 years, live and work in a regional area, state nominated or sponsored by an eligible family member. It can lead to permanent residence through subclass 191.

## Employer-sponsored visas
- Skills in Demand, subclass 482 (it replaced the Temporary Skill Shortage visa): temporary, needs a job offer from an approved Australian sponsor employer.
- Employer Nomination Scheme, subclass 186: permanent, the employer nominates the person.
- Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional, subclass 494: provisional, with a regional employer.
- No sponsor yet: the points-tested visas (189, 190, 491) don't need an employer. Employer visas need a job offer from an approved sponsor first. A consultant can say which fits the caller.
- Finding a sponsor: it means getting a job offer from an Australian employer who is willing to sponsor the role and is an approved sponsor. Usually that starts with applying for jobs in the caller's field in Australia. Which occupations and visas fit their profile is what a consultation covers.

## Study
- Student, subclass 500: needs enrolment in a registered course (a Confirmation of Enrolment), meeting the Genuine Student requirement, English, enough funds, and overseas student health cover.
- Temporary Graduate, subclass 485: after finishing eligible study in Australia, lets graduates live and work temporarily.

## Family
- Partner visas: onshore subclass 820, then permanent subclass 801; offshore subclass 309, then permanent subclass 100.
- Prospective Marriage, subclass 300: to come to Australia and marry.
- Parent visas, such as subclass 103 and 143: waits can be long; a consultant explains the options.

## Visiting and working holidays
- Visitor, subclass 600: tourism, visiting family, or short business visits.
- Working Holiday, subclass 417, and Work and Holiday, subclass 462: for young people (usually 18 to 30) from eligible countries.

## Other
- A bridging visa keeps someone lawful in Australia while their application is processed.
- If a caller names a subclass that isn't listed here, say you don't have details on that subclass to hand. Never guess what it is.
